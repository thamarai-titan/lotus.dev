# 1. Decision fatique

The is a real things when it comes to designing forms or user engagement needed parts

It like infront of a user you are stacking more choices means there is a chance to make no choice at all

magine you walk into a supermarket to buy pickle. There are 20 different varieties—mango, lemon, garlic, mixed vegetable, ginger, spicy, sweet, homemade, organic, regional brands, and many more.

Instead of making the decision quickly, you spend several minutes comparing prices, flavors, brands, and reviews. Eventually, you feel overwhelmed and either pick one at random or leave without buying anything.

Now imagine the store only displays the 6 most popular pickle varieties. You can quickly compare them, choose one with confidence, and complete your purchase in less time.

# 2. Smart Defaults

Like Studies in behavioral design reveal that over 95% of users stick to default settings on websites, browsers, and software. Because evaluating options requires effort, people overwhelmingly accept pre-selected configurations, a phenomenon known as the "default bias".

So we need to make choices based on user personas and giving them a smart default choice is more important in good designing.

# 3. Goal Gradiant Effect

Its basically like when you are about to finish a task your brain will pump more dopamine and a slight euphoria. which makes the person to finish the task very fastly.

So in designs we can use this effect to make users engaged in our design.

# 5. Endowment Effect

A software company gives you a 30-day free trial.

After using it every day, it starts to feel like your software, with your settings and your data.

When the trial ends, you're much more likely to pay for it because you don't want to lose something you now feel you own.

This is what the effect cause it makes the user feels like they are using it actually and more likely to buy it.
